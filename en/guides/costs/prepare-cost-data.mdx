Prepare cost data before interpreting variances. Cost analysis depends on complete master data and consistent operational records.

## Base data

<Steps>
  <Step title="Review items">
    In **Catalog > Items**, open each item and complete **Planned total cost per unit** on the **Costs** tab.

    In the planning view, also define the procurement source and stock rules.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Review suppliers and purchases">
    Keep prices, orders and receipts up to date for purchased materials.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Review employees">
    In **People > Employees**, open each employee and complete **Hourly cost** in **Information** if you will include their time in the calculation.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Review resources">
    In **Manufacturing > Resources**, open each workstation and configure its costs on the **Costs** tab if they should contribute to actual cost.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Review recipes">
    Ensure inputs, quantities, time and outputs are consistent before manufacturing.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Records that contribute to actual cost

| Record | Impact |
| --- | --- |
| Purchase receipts | Contribute the cost of purchased materials. |
| Manufacturing consumption | Determines the materials actually used. |
| Completed production | Distributes cost over manufactured output. |
| Operation time records | Contribute labor or resource cost. |
| Rejects and scrap | Explain quality or performance variances. |
| Stock counts and adjustments | Correct stock, but need clear justification. |

<Warning>
  If consumption, time records, prices or lots are missing, actual cost may give an incomplete picture. Review traceability before making decisions.
</Warning>

## Before comparing planned and actual costs

<Steps>
  <Step title="Select the period">
    Analyze a range with closed operations and recorded receipts.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Exclude incomplete data">
    Review open orders, partial receipts or pending stock counts.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Check large variances">
    Check whether they come from price, quantity, time, rejects or stock adjustments.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Document the cause">
    Add operational comments when a variance has a real explanation.
  </Step>
</Steps>
