**Assignments** prepare an operation for execution. They indicate where, when and with which resources it will run.

**Operation groups** bring together several operations for execution as an operational block when they share a work context.

## Assignments

An operation meets the minimum planning requirements when it has a workstation, a target date and any required tool. Stock status separately indicates material availability.

| Data | Use |
| --- | --- |
| Workstation | Place where the operation runs. |
| Target date | Reference for planning and prioritization. |
| Required tools | Auxiliary resources needed when the operation requires a characteristic. |
| Stock status | Indicates whether expected materials are available. |

The station, date and tool can come from a manual action, sequencing or an applied production plan. The plan does not assign employees: an employee's operational assignment is recorded when starting work or assigning them to a group.

## Operation groups

An operation group lets you work on several operations together. It is useful when they share a station, employee, materials, setup or pace of progress.

| Case | Why use a group |
| --- | --- |
| Several orders run in the same setup. | Avoids starting and pausing operations one by one. |
| An employee works on a batch of similar operations. | Centralizes progress and pauses. |
| Several operations share material availability. | Helps you review stock and checks together. |
| The shop floor works in cells or work queues. | Better reflects how actual work is executed. |

A group does not remove individual operations. Each operation retains its traceability, progress, consumption and checks.

Group actions do not apply individual start and quantity restrictions, including when executed from work mode. Creating the group or adding operations requires manufacturing order write permission; the operator can then work with the existing group. When creating a group or adding an operation, review the conditions shown in the dialog before confirming. Grouping is a management decision about how to execute that work together.

You need at least two operations to create a group. All must be **Planned** or **Paused**, have a pending quantity, belong to the same station and not belong to another group.

The group adjusts when one of its operations is no longer available:

- If you cancel the manufacturing order of a grouped operation, the operation leaves the group and the group's total quantity decreases. The group dissolves when it has no operations left. If the group is **Planned** or **Paused**, it also dissolves when only one operation remains.
- If you delete a grouped operation, Bold ejects it from the group first. You can only delete it while the group is **Planned**.

To take an operation out of its group without cancelling or deleting it, use **Eject from the group** in the group's operations or on the operation's page. The operation keeps existing on its own.

Progress recorded in the group stays pooled until an operation leaves the group or the group finishes. When an operation leaves earlier:

- The group keeps the progress that fits within the quantity of the remaining operations. Any excess goes to the ejected operation.
- If you select grouped operations in a manufacturing order's operations and use **Finish** or **Record and finish**, each one leaves the group and takes the group progress that corresponds to it, up to its quantity. It is then finished like the rest. This also works while the group is **In progress**.
- If the group dissolves because only one operation remains, that operation receives the group's remaining progress.
- You cannot dissolve a group with several operations and recorded progress, because Bold cannot tell which operation each unit belongs to. Finish the group with a breakdown, or eject operations until it dissolves.

## Common statuses

| Status | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Planned** | The group exists and can be prepared for execution. |
| **In progress** | Work has started. |
| **Paused** | Work is temporarily stopped. |
| **Finished** | The group has ended, and operations retain their history. |

Group statuses help you operate on the shop floor. Each operation's statuses continue to explain its actual progress.

Read [Manufacturing statuses](/en/concepts/manufacturing/manufacturing-statuses) to compare groups, operations, orders and plans.

## Relationship with work mode

In [Work mode](/en/concepts/work-mode), the operator sees active, recent or available operations according to the employee, station, area and assignments.

When they start, pause, resume or record progress, Bold updates execution and preserves who performed each action.

If work stops, record a [pause reason](/en/concepts/manufacturing/pause-reasons-and-interruptions) to explain the interruption.

## Design rules

- Assign a station before executing the operation.
- Select tools when the operation requires specific characteristics.
- Group only operations your team actually executes together.
- Review checks and stock before starting critical work.
- Do not use groups to hide operations that should be measured separately.
